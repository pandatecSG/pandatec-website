How to Choose an Ergonomic Office Chair in Singapore

A practical buying guide for offices, schools and facilities: the adjustments, standards (SS 514, EN 1335, BIFMA X5.1) and materials to compare before you order chairs in volume.

By PANDA COLLECTIONS procurement team · Updated November 3, 2026 · 8-minute read

Quick answer

An ergonomic office chair for a Singapore workplace should let each user set seat height (SS 514 recommends 35–50 cm with a desk of about 70 cm), seat depth, lumbar height and armrest height, recline the backrest to 100–120°, and stand on a five-caster swivel base. For buying, look for adjustment ranges that meet EN 1335-1 Type A or B, structural testing to ANSI/BIFMA X5.1, and a breathable mesh back for long desk shifts.

Why the right office chair matters for a business

Work-related musculoskeletal disorders (WRMSDs) are one of the top three types of occupational diseases in Singapore, and the Workplace Safety and Health Council estimated their economic loss at $3.5 billion in 2011, about 1% of GDP. A chair cannot prevent every back or neck complaint, but a chair that fits the user, combined with a correctly set desk and screen, is one of the simplest controls an employer can buy. For a procurement team, the goal is a chair that fits the widest range of staff, survives years of daily use, and can be documented against recognised standards.

Step 1: Profile the users and the work

Before comparing models, write down who will sit in the chair and how:

  • Sitting time: full-day desk work (6–9 hours) needs the full set of adjustments; meeting rooms and training rooms need comfort for 1–3 hours and easy stacking or folding.
  • Height range of users: a chair assigned to one person can be chosen to fit them; hot-desking and shift work need the widest adjustment range and controls that are easy to reset.
  • Body weight: standard chairs are tested for users up to the 95th percentile; plan heavy-duty chairs for larger users (see the BIFMA section below).
  • Desk and screen set-up: fixed-height desks around 70–75 cm, height-adjustable desks, or counters each change the seat height range you need.
  • Environment: air-conditioned office, non-air-conditioned site office, reception, clinic or cleanroom affects the choice of upholstery and base material.

Step 2: Check the adjustments that matter

SS 514, the Singapore Standard Code of Practice for Office Ergonomics (initiated by the Ministry of Manpower), asks for chairs that are adjustable, stable and give good back support. In practice, compare these controls on every shortlisted model:

Step 3: Use EN 1335-1 to compare adjustment ranges

The European standard EN 1335-1:2020 (amended 2022) sets dimensions for office work chairs and groups them by how much they adjust. It is a convenient way to compare chairs from different factories on the same basis:

Type AX≥ 160 mm≥ 70 mm each≥ 100 mmVery wide user range, executive and hot-desk chairs

Type CMinimal requirements with few adjustmentsVisitor, meeting and short-stay seating

Armrests and headrests are optional in every type. When a supplier says a chair is “EN 1335 compliant”, ask which type and request the test report for the exact model.

Step 4: Check strength and durability – ANSI/BIFMA X5.1

ANSI/BIFMA X5.1 is the American National Standard for general-purpose office chairs. It evaluates safety, durability and structural adequacy with around 20 tests, including swivel, tilt, backrest and caster durability cycles. The current 2017 edition is designed around a user of up to 275 lb (about 125 kg), the 95th percentile male. For larger users, the separate ANSI/BIFMA X5.11 standard covers people above 253 lb and up to 400 lb.

Two points are often misunderstood in tenders:

  • BIFMA is not an ergonomic rating. A BIFMA-tested chair is strong and durable, but it can still be the wrong size for your users. Check adjustments (Step 2–3) as well.
  • Certification is model-specific. Do not assume every chair in a series carries the same report. Ask for the report number, test lab and model code. PANDA COLLECTIONS lists its BIFMA certified office chairs separately for this reason.

Step 6: Match chair types to rooms

  • Workstations (6+ hours): ergonomic chairs or mesh task chairs with seat depth, lumbar and 3D/4D armrest adjustment.
  • Management and meeting rooms: PU leather chairs with synchro-tilt and a matching visitor version.
  • Training and multipurpose rooms: training chairs with writing tablets, folding seats or stacking frames.
  • Labs, cleanrooms and electronics assembly: ESD chairs and stools with conductive castors or glides.

Step 7: Set up the workstation, not just the chair

SS 514 treats the chair as part of the workstation. Use these checks when the chairs arrive:

  1. Set the seat so the feet rest flat and the forearms are level with a work surface of about 70 cm.
  2. Place the keyboard and mouse at the same level, close to the body.
  3. Set the top edge of the monitor no higher than eye level.
  4. Recline the backrest slightly (100–120°) and adjust lumbar support to the lower back.
  5. Encourage short pauses; SS 514 guidance notes that breaks of 20–30 seconds may enhance productivity.

Step 8: Procurement checklist

  • Trial 2–3 shortlisted models with your shortest and tallest users before ordering in volume.
  • Request the model code, dimension drawing, EN 1335 type and BIFMA X5.1 (or X5.11) report where required.
  • Confirm warranty terms for the gas lift, mechanism, base and upholstery, and the availability of spare parts.
  • Specify castors for your floor type and any colour or fabric code.
  • Plan delivery, assembly and removal of old chairs in the same quotation.

Frequently asked questions

What seat height should an office chair have in Singapore?

SS 514, the Singapore code of practice for office ergonomics, gives a recommended chair height between 35 cm and 50 cm, used with a work surface of about 70 cm. Choose a chair whose gas lift covers the shortest and tallest users at your desks.

Is a BIFMA-certified chair the same as an ergonomic chair?

No. ANSI/BIFMA X5.1 tests the safety, durability and structural strength of general-purpose office chairs. It does not measure ergonomic fit. Check adjustment ranges (for example EN 1335-1 Type A or B) and trial the chair with real users.

What weight is a standard office chair tested for?

The 2017 edition of ANSI/BIFMA X5.1 is designed around users up to 275 lb (about 125 kg), the 95th percentile male. For heavier users, ask for chairs tested to ANSI/BIFMA X5.11, which covers users above 253 lb and up to 400 lb.

Mesh or PU leather: which is better for Singapore offices?

Mesh backs let air pass through and suit long desk shifts in a warm, humid climate. PU leather wipes clean easily and suits reception, management and shared or public-facing areas. Many offices use mesh task chairs for staff and leather chairs for meeting and director rooms.

How far should an office chair recline?

SS 514 guidance recommends a slightly reclined backrest at 100 to 120 degrees to reduce pressure on the spine. A synchro-tilt mechanism with tension control and a recline lock lets each user set this angle.
