How to Choose a Dining Table Set for Restaurants, Canteens and Offices

Standard heights, space per diner, aisle clearances, tabletop materials and durability standards to check before you order dining tables, chairs and bar stools for a commercial space.

By PANDA COLLECTIONS procurement team · Updated November 3, 2026 · 8-minute read

Quick answer

For commercial dining, use tables about 72–76 cm (29–30 in) high with chairs whose seats are about 46 cm (18 in) high, leaving 25–30 cm between the seat and the underside of the table. Allow about 60 cm (24 in) of table edge per diner for casual dining and 76 cm (30 in) for fine dining, and keep at least 91 cm (36 in) behind chairs for people to pass. Choose a tabletop that resists heat, scratches and cleaning chemicals, and ask for tables tested to EN 15372 and chairs tested to EN 16139 for non-domestic use.

Step 1: Match the furniture to the venue

  • Quick-service restaurants and food courts: high turnover and frequent cleaning; choose compact tables, stackable or fixed seating and wipe-clean surfaces.
  • Casual and fine dining: more space per guest, upholstered chairs or banquette and booth seating, and tables that can be joined for groups.
  • Staff canteens and schools: durable PP plastic chairs and long tables that are easy to clean and rearrange.
  • Hotels and serviced apartments: a consistent look between restaurant, lounge and room furniture.
  • Office pantries and breakout areas: mixed heights, such as standard tables plus a counter with stools.

Step 2: Use standard heights

Table typeTable heightMatching seat height
Dining table72–76 cm (29–30 in)Dining chair seat 43–46 cm (17–18 in)
Counter-height table91 cm (36 in)Counter stool seat 58–61 cm (23–24 in)
Bar or pub table107 cm (42 in)Bar stool seat 74–76 cm (29–30 in)

Keep 25–30 cm (10–12 in) between the seat and the underside of the table so guests can cross their legs, and at least 15 cm (6 in) between an armrest and the table underside so armchairs slide in. Browse dining tables, dining chairs and bar stools.

Step 3: Plan space per diner and clearances

Start from the floor plan: mark service routes, doors and kitchen pass first, then fill the remaining area with tables. Mixing two-seat tables that can be joined gives more flexibility than fixed large tables.

Step 4: Choose the tabletop material

MaterialStrengthsBest for
High-pressure laminate (HPL) or compact laminateWide colour choice; good scratch and stain resistance; cost-effectiveCanteens, cafés, office pantries
Solid wood or wood veneerWarm, premium look; can be refinishedRestaurants, hotel dining; protect from heat and moisture

For outdoor or semi-outdoor dining in Singapore's climate, choose UV-stable plastics, powder-coated aluminium or treated teak; see outdoor furniture.

Step 5: Check bases, frames and durability

  • Bases: pedestal bases free up leg room at small tables; four-leg frames are more stable for large tables; levelling feet handle uneven floors.
  • Chair frames: welded steel or aluminium frames and reinforced PP shells suit high-turnover venues; check stackability for storage.
  • Standards: EN 15372 sets strength, durability and safety requirements for non-domestic tables; EN 16139 does the same for non-domestic seating. Ask which standard each model has been tested to and request the report.
  • Cleaning: confirm the finish tolerates the detergents your team uses daily.

Procurement checklist

  1. Measure the room and mark service routes and clearances.
  2. Decide the seat count and mix of two-, four- and six-seat tables.
  3. Select table and seat heights that match (dining, counter or bar).
  4. Choose tabletop and upholstery materials for your cleaning routine.
  5. Ask for samples or a trial set before ordering in volume.
  6. Confirm test standards, warranty, spare parts and lead time.

Frequently asked questions

What is the standard height of a dining table?

A standard dining table is about 72–76 cm (29–30 in) high and is used with chairs whose seats are about 43–46 cm (17–18 in) high.

How much space does each diner need?

Allow about 60 cm (24 in) of table edge per person for casual dining and 76 cm (30 in) or more for fine dining.

How much space should be left behind dining chairs?

Allow 91–152 cm (36–60 in) of total clearance behind a seated chair, and 137–152 cm (54–60 in) for an accessible route.

What height should bar stools be?

Bar stools with a seat of 74–76 cm (29–30 in) suit 107 cm (42 in) bar tables; counter stools with a 58–61 cm (23–24 in) seat suit 91 cm (36 in) counters.

Which tabletop is best for a staff canteen?

High-pressure or compact laminate is a cost-effective choice with good scratch and stain resistance and easy daily cleaning.

Which standards apply to commercial dining furniture?

EN 15372 covers strength, durability and safety of non-domestic tables and EN 16139 covers non-domestic seating. Ask for test reports for the exact models.
