PANDA COLLECTIONS, the curated procurement brand of PANDATEC PTE. LTD. (Singapore, est. 2015), supports B2B buyers with chemical sourcing for manufacturing, packaging, construction, agriculture, water treatment, electronics and industrial maintenance. The range is organised in eleven groups: plastic raw materials and synthetic resins, industrial solvents and basic chemicals, petrochemical raw materials, fertilizers and agrochemicals, coatings, resins and adhesive raw materials, specialty chemicals and industrial consumables, polyurethane raw materials and systems, superabsorbent polymers, aroma chemicals, battery materials, and cosmetic and personal care raw materials.

A useful chemical enquiry names the product and, where possible, its CAS number, grade, purity or key specification, application, quantity and unit (kg, MT, drums, IBC or containers), packaging, destination port or delivery address, required delivery date, preferred Incoterms and the documents you need, such as SDS, TDS or COA.

PANDA COLLECTIONS identifies qualified manufacturers and supply options, checks documents and product identity, coordinates samples, quality checks and supplier communication, and arranges logistics to Singapore or onward export. Product availability, origin, specification, dangerous-goods transport, import permits and lead time are confirmed for each enquiry; the catalogue is a starting point for sourcing, not a statement of stock.

A PRACTICAL SOURCING CHECKLIST

Specify the chemistry, grade and supply conditions

Two products with the same generic name may differ in composition, grade, impurity limits, performance and regulatory classification. For a comparable quotation, identify the intended application and request the exact manufacturer specification. Where relevant, include CAS Registry Number, concentration or composition, grade, purity, key limits (such as moisture, colour, viscosity or particle size), and the required test method. These details help suppliers confirm whether an offered product matches the process.

What to defineUseful enquiry details
Identity & performanceProduct name, CAS number (if applicable), grade, composition, purity, critical limits, intended process and any approved equivalent criteria.
Quantity & packagingTrial or annual volume; kg, metric tonnes or units; bags, drums, IBCs or bulk; palletisation, container compatibility and storage conditions.
Quality documentsCurrent SDS, TDS and batch-specific COA where available; specification revision, sample requirement, shelf life and traceability expectations.
Delivery & trade termsDestination, requested delivery window, preferred Incoterms rule and named place, plus whether the shipment is hazardous or temperature-sensitive.

Documents, handling and Singapore controls

Before purchase, align the product identity and intended use with the supplier documents. A Safety Data Sheet (SDS) communicates hazards and safe handling information; a Technical Data Sheet (TDS) describes typical product properties or use conditions; a Certificate of Analysis (COA) reports test results for a particular batch. Ask which revision and batch the documents cover, and confirm any customer-specific acceptance tests before placing an order.

Regulatory requirements depend on the substance, concentration, activity and destination. In Singapore, some hazardous substances are controlled under the Environmental Protection and Management Act and may require licences, permits or transport approval. Certain scheduled chemicals are also subject to Chemical Weapons Convention controls administered by Singapore Customs. Buyers and importers should verify CAS numbers, HS codes, mixture concentrations, end use and the relevant approvals with the competent authority or a qualified compliance professional before shipment. PANDA COLLECTIONS can help coordinate supplier information and logistics; it does not replace the buyer’s regulatory assessment.

For a sourcing review, send the specification or SDS, target quantity and delivery location through the business quotation form. See our global sourcing, quality control and international logistics support.

What chemicals can PANDA COLLECTIONS source?

PANDA COLLECTIONS reviews B2B sourcing enquiries for commodity and engineering plastics (PE, PP, PVC, PET, ABS), industrial solvents (methanol, ethanol, acetone, toluene, xylene, ethyl acetate), petrochemical feedstocks (propylene, ethane, ammonia, aromatics), fertilizers (urea, ammonium sulphate, DAP, potassium chloride), coating and adhesive raw materials (epoxy, polyurethane, acrylic resins, pigments) and specialty chemicals such as water-treatment, cleaning, metalworking and electronic chemicals.

Do you supply chemicals outside Singapore?

Yes. PANDA COLLECTIONS is based in Singapore and can coordinate supply to Singapore and to buyers in Southeast Asia and other markets, subject to the product classification, dangerous-goods rules, import permits and registration requirements of the destination country.

What documents come with a chemical quotation?

Depending on the product and manufacturer, a quotation can be supported by a Safety Data Sheet (SDS), Technical Data Sheet (TDS), Certificate of Analysis (COA), product specification, packing details and certificate of origin. Required documents should be stated in the enquiry.

Do you show chemical prices on the website?

No. Chemical prices move with feedstock markets and depend on grade, purity, packaging, quantity, Incoterms and destination. Send your requirement and PANDA COLLECTIONS will reply with a customised quotation.

Which specifications should I include in a chemical enquiry?

Share the product name and CAS number where applicable, grade, composition or purity, intended application, critical limits, target quantity, packaging, destination and required delivery date. Attach the current specification or SDS if available and state whether samples, a batch COA or specific test methods are required.

Can you provide SDS, TDS and a batch COA?

Document availability varies by product and manufacturer. State the documents you need before quotation; request the applicable SDS and TDS and confirm whether a batch-specific COA, sample or additional test report can be supplied for the quoted grade.

Do all industrial chemicals need a Singapore import licence?

No single answer applies to every chemical. Some substances and activities are controlled, and requirements can depend on identity, concentration, quantity, intended activity and transport.
